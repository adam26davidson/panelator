## Question

How do teams measure whether agent-driven development is working, and which metrics have been shown to drive good decisions about autonomy? Specifically: (1) which published metric sets exist for AI-assisted and agent-driven engineering (DORA, SPACE, GitHub Copilot metrics, Anthropic and OpenAI usage reporting, academic studies with data), with each metric's definition and what its source claims it predicts; (2) for each candidate metric in process-spec section 7, whether anyone has defined and used it, under what name, with what result; (3) whether any published scheme lets a class of change earn less human review based on measured outcomes (autonomy ladders), in dependency automation, progressive delivery, or agent-specific writing; (4) how metrics are actually collected from GitHub, CI and harness output, so the thinking run knows what is cheap; (5) Goodhart effects and gaming reported for these metrics.

## Findings

### 1. Published metric sets

**F1.** [verified] DORA now defines five software delivery metrics, split into throughput and instability. Throughput: *change lead time* ("the amount of time it takes for a change to go from committed to version control to deployed in production"), *deployment frequency* ("the number of deployments over a given period or the time between deployments"), *failed deployment recovery time* ("the time it takes to recover from a deployment that fails and requires immediate intervention"). Instability: *change fail rate* ("the ratio of deployments that require immediate intervention following a deployment"), *deployment rework rate* ("the ratio of deployments that are unplanned but happen as a result of an incident in production"). Source: https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys/ (updated 2026-01-05). Note for the spec: DORA's "rework rate" is a deployment-level metric and is not the same thing as process-spec section 7's "rework rate (fix cycles per PR)".

**F2.** [verified] DORA's 2025 report (published 2025-09-23, "nearly 5,000 technology professionals" surveyed, 90% AI adoption) finds that "AI adoption is now linked to higher software delivery throughput". DORA's own 2026-03-10 insight states the full result: "higher AI adoption is associated with an increase in both software delivery throughput and software delivery instability", and attributes the tension to verification overhead ("the time saved during initial code or content generation is often re-allocated to verification overhead"; "velocity gains for an individual author frequently translate into a significantly increased cognitive load for the reviewer") and recommends working in small batches. Sources: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/dora-report-2025/; https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/; https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/ ("AI improves throughput, but often at the cost of stability if your foundation isn't solid"). No effect sizes are given on the open pages; the PDF is gated.

**F3.** [verified, names unverified] DORA published an "AI Capabilities Model" (report page dated 2025-11-25) described as "a practical guide to the seven capabilities that amplify the benefits of AI", and says "for each of the seven core capabilities, this report details implementation strategies, specific tactics for teams to get started, and methods for monitoring progress". The seven names are not listed on the open page; DORA's own pages confirm "working in small batches", version control, a communicated AI policy and user-centricity are among them (https://dora.dev/capabilities/working-in-small-batches/, https://dora.dev/capabilities/version-control/, https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/report/). Full list: not verified.

**F4.** [verified] DORA (2026-06-02) explicitly recommends, for AI programmes, pairing the delivery metrics with "efficiency and risk indicators (think cost per accepted change and code rework rates)" and measuring "measurable teamwide outcomes" rather than individual AI usage. Source: https://dora.dev/insights/finding-balance-in-the-era-of-tokenmaxxing/. This is the closest published endorsement of two of the spec's candidate metrics (cost per merged PR; rework).

**F5.** [unverified, secondary] A DORA "ROI of AI-assisted Software Development" report (2026.01) is reported by InfoQ (https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/05/dora-roi-ai-assisted-dev-report/) to introduce a J-curve of adoption and a "verification tax" and to argue code review becomes the critical control point. DORA's own page (https://dora.dev/ai/) confirms the report exists but gives no content; the PDF is gated.

**F6.** [partially verified] SPACE (Forsgren, Storey, Maddila, Zimmermann, Houck, Butler; ACM Queue, February 2021) exists and its core premise is on Microsoft Research's page: "Developer productivity is about more than an individual's activity levels or the efficiency of the engineering systems relied on to ship software, and it cannot be measured by a single metric or dimension." The five dimension names (Satisfaction and well-being, Performance, Activity, Communication and collaboration, Efficiency and flow) and the recommendation to use at least three dimensions including a perceptual measure are from training knowledge, not from a source read today; the ACM Queue body returned 403. Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/.

**F7.** [verified] GitHub Copilot metrics are delivered as downloadable daily and 28-day reports (NDJSON) at enterprise, organisation, repository and user level via `GET /orgs/{org}/copilot/metrics/reports/...` endpoints; reports exist from 2025-10-10, history is kept for one year, and the policy "Copilot usage metrics" must be enabled. The schema includes `code_generation_activity_count`, `code_acceptance_activity_count`, `loc_added_sum`, `loc_deleted_sum`, `loc_suggested_to_add_sum`, `user_initiated_interaction_count`, `ai_credits_used`, `totals_by_feature` with feature values `code_completion`, `agent_edit`, `code_review_active`, `code_review_passive`, `cloud_agent`, and a repository-level `pull_requests` block with `total_created`, `total_merged`, `total_reviewed`, `total_created_by_copilot`, `total_reviewed_by_copilot`, `median_minutes_to_merge`, plus `pull_request_review_times`. Sources: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/copilot/copilot-metrics; https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/reference/copilot-usage-metrics/example-schema. These are org/enterprise products; a single-maintainer repo on a personal account cannot use them.

**F8.** [verified] GitHub's own guidance on what Copilot metrics predict is deliberately weak: comparisons between engaged and passive users are "directional associations, not proof that a change in adoption caused a change in output", and "differences in team composition, project complexity, or seniority ... can influence the comparison as much as Copilot adoption itself". Its rollout tutorial recommends adoption, engagement, self-reported productivity and enablement metrics and does not recommend PR throughput, cycle time, CI success or bug rates. Sources: https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/reference/interpret-copilot-metrics; https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/roll-out-at-scale/measure-success.

**F9.** [verified] Anthropic's Usage and Cost Admin API (`GET /v1/organizations/usage_report/messages`, `GET /v1/organizations/cost_report`) returns uncached input, cached input, cache-creation and output tokens, grouped by `api_key_id`, `workspace_id`, `model`, `service_tier`, `context_window`, `inference_geo`, `speed`, at `1m`/`1h`/`1d` buckets (max 1,440/168/31 buckets per call); cost is USD in cents at daily granularity; data appears within about 5 minutes; polling once per minute is supported. It requires an Admin API key and "the Admin API is unavailable for individual accounts". Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/usage-cost-api.

**F10.** [verified] Anthropic's Claude Code Analytics API (`GET /v1/organizations/usage_report/claude_code?starting_at=YYYY-MM-DD`) returns, per user per day: `num_sessions`, `lines_of_code.added/removed`, `commits_by_claude_code`, `pull_requests_by_claude_code`, per-tool `accepted/rejected` counts (`edit_tool`, `multi_edit_tool`, `write_tool`, `notebook_edit_tool`), and per-model `tokens.input/output/cache_read/cache_creation` with `estimated_cost.amount` in cents; `customer_type` is `api` or `subscription`; data lags up to one hour; free; Admin API only (so also unavailable to individual accounts); excludes Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry. Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/claude-code-analytics-api. These are the vendor's chosen "productivity" metrics: activity counts, not outcomes.

**F11.** [partially verified] OpenAI's Usage API (`GET /v1/organization/usage/completions`) returns `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `input_cached_tokens`, `num_model_requests` per bucket, grouped by `project_id`, `user_id`, `api_key_id`, `model`, `batch`; the Costs API (`GET /v1/organization/costs`) returns `amount.value`/`currency` per `line_item`/`project_id`, daily only; both need an organisation Admin key. Verified from the cookbook (January 2025) at https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/completions_usage_api; the `service_tier` group and the bucket limits (31/168/1440) come from search snippets of the reference and are unverified. Whether Codex usage billed to a ChatGPT subscription appears here: not found.

**F12.** [verified] METR's RCT (2025-07-10; 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 issues, Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet) measured that "when developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues", while developers forecast a 24% speedup and afterwards "still believed AI had sped them up by 20%". The authors restrict generalisation to experienced developers on their own mature repositories. Source: https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/.

**F13.** [verified] Two controlled experiments found speedups: Google's enterprise RCT (96 engineers, one complex task) estimated "about 21%" less time on task "with a large confidence interval" and warned the effect may not transfer across tools or time (arXiv:2410.12944); the GitHub Copilot experiment found the treatment group "completed the task 55.8% faster" on an HTTP-server task (arXiv:2302.06590). Together with F12, the measured direction of AI's effect on time depends on task and population; time-on-task alone is not a stable success metric.

**F14.** [verified] The AIDev dataset (Li, Zhang, Hassan, 2025-07-20) holds "over 456,000 pull requests by five leading agents - OpenAI Codex, Devin, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Claude Code - across 61,000 repositories" with "review timelines, code changes, and integration outcomes", and reports that "although agents often outperform humans in speed, their PRs are accepted less frequently". Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15003. This is the only public dataset found with agent-authored PR outcomes at scale.

**F15.** [verified] Ehsani et al. (MSR 2026) on 33,596 agent PRs: 71.48% merged overall; by agent Codex 82.59%, Cursor 65.22%, Claude Code 59.04%, Devin 53.76%, Copilot 43.04%; by task "documentations (84%), CI (79%), and build (74%)" merge most, "performance (55%) and fix (64%)" least. Not-merged PRs are larger (17% more lines modified) and "tend to experience more CI failures" (Cliff's delta 0.24); "each additional CI failure decreases the odds of a merge by about 15%". Of 600 rejected PRs: reviewer abandonment 38%, duplicate PRs 23%, CI/test failures 17%, unwanted features 4%, agentic issues 2%. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15195.

**F16.** [verified] Abujadallah et al. (MSR 2026) on AIDev bug fixes: "46.41% of the fixes proposed by the agents Copilot, Devin, Cursor, and Claude are rejected"; 14 reasons in four categories: incorrect implementation (incomplete, wrong approach), CI and test failures, implementation inability (no code generated, lost sessions), low-priority fixes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13468.

**F17.** [verified] Ziegler et al. (2022) found that "the rate with which shown suggestions are accepted, rather than more specific metrics regarding the persistence of completions in the code over time, drives developers' perception of productivity". Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06537. Acceptance rate is therefore a perception metric, which is also how GitHub frames it (F8).

**F18.** [verified] Google's five-year technical-debt research (Jaspan and Green, IEEE Software 2023) measured debt through quarterly engineering surveys and found that code and process metrics did not reliably predict survey-reported technical debt; management used a maturity model. Source: https://research.google/pubs/defining-measuring-and-managing-technical-debt/.

### 2. Process-spec section 7 candidates, metric by metric

**F19.** [verified] *Rework rate (fix cycles per PR).* No published metric under this name was found. Nearest: DORA's "deployment rework rate" (F1, different definition); DORA's "code rework rates" recommendation (F4, undefined); the AIDev studies' review-dynamics and CI-failure counts (F15). kari-website used fix-cycle count as a control rule, not a recorded metric: "if the SAME feedback substantially survives two consecutive fix cycles on one PR ... dispatch a plan agent ... If that cycle also fails, leave the PR for a human" (`automation/agents/issue-pipeline.md` lines 800-806). Nothing in `dispatch.sh` or the README stores the count.

**F20.** [verified] *Spec deviation.* Not found as a named metric. Nearest published categories: "unwanted features" (4% of rejections) and "agent misalignment"/"agentic issues" (2%) in F15; "incorrect implementation (incomplete, wrong approach)" in F16. The 2026 spec-driven-development paper (arXiv:2609.00252) frames specifications as "the contract substrate between humans and agents" but proposes no metric and says its evidence base is immature.

**F21.** [verified] *Spec churn (edits after approval).* The established name is *requirements volatility*. NASA SWE-200 (NPR 7150.2D req. 5.4.6): "the project manager shall collect, track, and report software requirements volatility metrics", counting requirements added, deleted, modified and unresolved TBD/TBR items, and "percentage of baseline requirements changed per phase"; the handbook expects early volatility and says post-PDR changes should be minimised. Source: https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVD/pages/102695533/SWE-200+-+Software+Requirements+Volatility+Metrics. No agent-specific use found.

**F22.** [verified] *Time to green per PR.* Not found as a named metric. Raw material exists: workflow-run `run_started_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `conclusion`, `run_attempt`, `head_sha` (F30); CI-failure count is the strongest single predictor of non-merge in F15. GitHub's org-level Actions metrics UI exposes usage and performance metrics with CSV export but, per its docs page, no API (F32).

**F23.** [verified] *Cost per merged PR by model and stage.* DORA recommends "cost per accepted change" (F4) without a method. Copilot reports `ai_credits_used` per user per day (F7); Claude Code Analytics reports estimated cost per user per day per model and PR counts, but not per PR (F10). kari-website records cost per *session* (`total_cost_usd`, per-model `modelUsage`) in `~/.local/state/kari-website-automation/usage/<agent>-<stamp>.json` (203 records on this machine; `dispatch.sh` lines 365-416) and sums them per agent in `--status` ("12 runs, $38.20 total, $3.18 mean (180d)", lines 419-432). A tick's session spans several PRs and subagent stages, so cost per PR by stage was never derivable from kari's records; the README says so indirectly: "whether background subagents are folded in is not something the dispatcher can verify, so compare `--status` totals against the subscription's usage page before trusting them as absolute" (`automation/README.md` lines 157-162). Nobody found publishes per-PR-per-stage cost.

**F24.** [verified] *Product vs machinery ratio.* Not found outside kari-website. kari measured it once, by hand, over three days: "Two thirds of the agent PRs merged 2026-08-19..21 were tooling for the pipeline's own environment; eight touched the website, and Phase C filed machinery issues faster than Phase B cleared them" (commit `75580b8`, 2026-08-21). The response was a prompt-level lean, "a direction for the orchestrator's judgement, not a quota", plus a label split: `tooling` (topic, machinery) vs `automation` (provenance, filed by the pipeline) (`issue-pipeline.md` lines 573-592; commit `3ca50b2` #408). No continuous measurement was built. Google's finding that metrics do not predict perceived debt (F18) is the closest external evidence that such ratios need a definition agreed in advance.

**F25.** [verified] *Defect escape rate.* The deployment analogue is DORA's change fail rate (F1). No agent-specific escape-rate study with data was found; the AIDev papers measure merge, not post-merge defects.

**F26.** [verified] *Human interventions per PR.* Not found as a named metric with data. Inverse evidence: 38% of rejected agent PRs failed through "reviewer abandonment", i.e. no human engaged at all (F15). The AWS graduated-autonomy scheme scores accuracy, safety, consistency, compliance and efficiency over a rolling 50-action window rather than counting interventions (F38). kari-website has a `needs-human` label and a Telegram alert path but no count of them (`issue-pipeline.md` lines 77-150; Agent 7 covers the actual counts).

**F27.** [verified] *Feature-to-debt ratio actually worked.* Not found as a metric. Google treats debt as survey-reported, not derivable from code or process metrics (F18). kari-website's 2:1-style lean existed only as prompt text (F24).

**F28.** [verified] *Queue age and lead time.* Established definitions: Kanban Guide v2025.5 defines *Work Item Age* as "the elapsed time between when a work item started and the current date", *Cycle Time* as "the elapsed time between when a work item started and when a work item finished", *Throughput* as "the number of work items finished per unit of time" (https://kanbanguides.org/english/); DORA's change lead time runs from commit to production (F1). GitHub's repository Copilot report carries `median_minutes_to_merge` (F7).

### 3. Autonomy ladders

**F29.** [verified] Renovate's automerge is a deployed, outcome-gated ladder by *risk class*, not by measured history: "Renovate will not automerge until it sees passing status checks / check runs for the branch"; `automergeType` is `pr` (default), `branch` ("if tests pass, Renovate pushes a commit directly to the base branch without PR. If tests fail, Renovate raises a PR for you to review") or `pr-comment`; `ignoreTests: true` disables the gate, with the warning "we strongly recommend you have tests in any project where you are regularly updating dependencies". The recommended graduation: `lockFileMaintenance` first ("the lowest risk type of update"), then `devDependencies`, lint tools, then minor/patch for SemVer ecosystems excluding pre-1.0 versions. Source: https://docs.renovatebot.com/key-concepts/automerge/ (Renovate 44.132.2).

**F30.** [verified] GitHub's Dependabot guidance is the same shape: `dependabot/fetch-metadata` exposes `update-type` (e.g. `version-update:semver-patch`), `dependency-type` (e.g. `direct:production`) and `dependency-names`; the sample workflow enables `gh pr merge --auto --merge` only for patch updates of a named dependency, and relies on "Require status checks to pass before merging" branch protection to make the gate real. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/dependabot/working-with-dependabot/automating-dependabot-with-github-actions. kari-website moved the other way once: "Require review for pre-1.0 minor bumps instead of auto-merging them (#525)" (commit `8c389cf`).

**F31.** [verified] Progressive-delivery tools gate promotion on measured outcomes with explicit thresholds. Argo Rollouts: an `AnalysisTemplate` lists `metrics` with `successCondition`/`failureCondition`, `interval`, `count`, `failureLimit`, `inconclusiveLimit`, `consecutiveErrorLimit` and (v1.8+) `consecutiveSuccessLimit`; a failed inline analysis aborts the rollout (automatic rollback), an inconclusive one pauses for a human. Flagger: default metrics `request-success-rate` and `request-duration`, `interval` (default 60s), `threshold` "max number of failed metric checks before rollback", `stepWeight`/`maxWeight`, and webhooks for `confirm-rollout`/`confirm-promotion` manual gates. Sources: https://argo-rollouts.readthedocs.io/en/stable/features/analysis/; https://docs.flagger.app/usage/how-it-works. These are the only schemes found where a numeric threshold on measured outcomes, not a human, decides promotion.

**F32.** [verified] Label-driven merge automation is mature in large OSS projects. Prow Tide merges when a PR carries all `labels` (Kubernetes uses `lgtm`, `approved`) and none of `missingLabels` (`do-not-merge/hold`, `needs-rebase`), with required status contexts from branch protection, batch testing and configurable `merge_method`. Mergify rules combine `author`, `label`, `files`, `check-success`, `#approved-reviews-by`, `dependabot-update-type`, `dependabot-dependency-type` and others. Both let a policy say "this author or this change class merges on green without human review" declaratively. Sources: https://docs.prow.k8s.io/docs/components/core/tide/config/; https://docs.mergify.com/configuration/conditions/.

**F33.** [verified] The one published agent-specific scheme where autonomy is *earned from measurements* is AWS's "graduated autonomy" (2026-08-26): four tiers by trust score (T1 Probation 0-40 "read and list only"; T2 Supervised 41-70 "human approves high-risk"; T3 Trusted 71-90 "anomalies flagged for review"; T4 Autonomous 91-100 "post-hoc audit only"); the score weighs accuracy 25%, safety 20%, consistency 20%, compliance 20%, efficiency 15% over a rolling 50-action window; promotion requires staying above threshold for the whole window plus a 5-point hysteresis; "demotion is immediate" on a safety-floor breach; "safety acts as an independent floor". It is a reference architecture; the post reports no deployment data. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/closing-the-ai-agent-trust-gap-with-graduated-autonomy/.

**F34.** [verified] A deployed small-scale instance: jturan/jade PR #19 (2026-09-20) adds per-unit grades `gated`, `merge`, `full` decided at plan time by a human, with hard blockers that override the grade: security-review flag, blocking review findings, tests not run ("nothing was demonstrated"), "it needed a retry", protected paths (migrations, auth, CI, secrets, deploy manifests), and a per-run cap `--max-auto-merges` (default 3); "a retried unit never auto-merges, even at `full`"; every auto-merge leaves an explanatory comment; deployment is excluded because "merging is reversible; deploying often is not". Source: https://github.com/jturan/jade/pull/19. No outcome data reported. This is a ladder by *risk class with per-item demotion signals*, not by measured history.

**F35.** [unverified, vendor] Codacy describes a four-level ladder (level 3 "bounded": agent opens PR, merge needs checks plus a person; level 4 "autonomous": unattended, checked afterwards) and says autonomy "gets earned through a clean track record". Source: https://blog.codacy.com/how-codacy-dogfoods-verity-with-its-own-ai-agents, not followed to data.

**F36.** [verified, by absence] No source was found in which a class of *code change* moved up a ladder because a measured rework or escape rate crossed a threshold. Every deployed scheme found gates on (a) risk class decided in advance (F29, F30, F34), (b) labels applied by humans or bots (F32), or (c) live production metrics for a *deployment* (F31). Measured-history promotion exists only as the AWS reference design (F33) and vendor prose (F35).

### 4. Collection mechanics

**F37.** [verified] GitHub REST, API version `2026-03-10`. The PR *list* endpoint omits `merged`, `merged_by`, `review_comments`, `comments`, `commits`, `additions`, `deletions`, `changed_files`; those appear only on `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls/{pull_number}`, so per-PR size and comment counts cost one request per PR. Timestamps `created_at`, `updated_at`, `closed_at`, `merged_at` are on both. `per_page` max 100. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/pulls/pulls.

**F38.** [verified] Review cycles come from `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls/{pull_number}/reviews`: each review has `state` (the doc lists `APPROVED`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` [sic, as read], `COMMENTED`, `DISMISSED`, `PENDING`), `submitted_at`, `commit_id`, `user`. Counting reviews with a changes-requested state per PR, or distinct `commit_id` values reviewed, gives fix cycles. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/pulls/reviews.

**F39.** [verified] Label history and stage timing come from `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/issues/{issue_number}/timeline`, which returns `labeled`/`unlabeled` events with `label.name`, `actor`, `created_at`, plus `reviewed`, `review_requested`, `committed`, `cross-referenced`, `merged`, `closed`, sub-issue and blocked-by events; `per_page` max 100 with page-based pagination; an `exclude` parameter filters event types. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/issues/timeline. This is the single endpoint from which queue age per label state, time from label to PR, and human-vs-bot actors can all be derived.

**F40.** [verified] CI timing comes from `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/actions/runs` filtered by `head_sha`, `branch`, `event`, `status`, `created` (search-syntax date range), with `run_started_at`, `updated_at`, `conclusion`, `run_attempt`; the per-run `/timing` endpoint (billable ms by OS, `run_duration_ms`) "is being deprecated" per the page. Jobs carry `started_at`/`completed_at`. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/actions/workflow-runs. The organisation Actions metrics UI offers up to 100-day custom ranges and CSV export; its page mentions no API. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/actions/administering-github-actions/viewing-github-actions-metrics-for-your-organization.

**F41.** [verified] The Search API is the wrong tool for complete listings: "the GitHub REST API provides up to 1,000 results for each search", `incomplete_results` is set to `true` on timeout, and authenticated search is limited to 30 requests per minute. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/search/search. This bears directly on the completeness principle in process-spec 6.4: `total_count` can exceed what is retrievable, so the assert-against-total check must use the list endpoints, not search.

**F42.** [verified] Claude Code headless (`claude -p --output-format json`) emits one result object with `result`, `session_id`, `is_error`, `num_turns`, `duration_ms`, `duration_api_ms`, `total_cost_usd`, `usage` (`input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, ...), `modelUsage` keyed by model with `inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `cacheReadInputTokens`, `cacheCreationInputTokens`, `costUSD`, and (with `--permission-prompts none`) `permission_denials`; exit code is 0 on success, non-zero on failure, 143 on SIGTERM. Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless. The kari-website record on this machine confirms the field names and adds `usage.iterations`, `usage.service_tier`, `usage.speed`, `usage.fallback_credit`, `usage.inference_geo` (`~/.local/state/kari-website-automation/usage/issue-pipeline-20261002T115254.json`: `total_cost_usd` 11.15, `num_turns` 22, `duration_ms` 1,903,093, models `claude-fable-5-1` and `claude-opus-5-5`).

**F43.** [verified] Three caveats on those numbers from Anthropic's own docs: (a) "`total_cost_usd` and `costUSD` fields are client-side estimates, not authoritative billing data"; (b) `usage` "counts only the top-level agent loop, so tokens consumed inside subagents are not added", while `total_cost_usd` and `modelUsage` include subagents, so stage-level cost needs `modelUsage` or the `stream-json` stream with `parent_tool_use_id`; (c) since v2.1.277 a resumed session's result "already includes the session's earlier spend", so summing results across `--resume` calls double-counts. `error_max_budget_usd` results omit the over-budget response from `usage` but not from `total_cost_usd`; a crashed session's final result "may carry zeroed" cost fields. Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/cost-tracking. kari-website's dispatcher already handles (c)'s precondition by using a fresh session per tick, and handles crashes by writing no record: "a record that cannot be summed is worse than a gap" (`dispatch.sh` lines 372-376).

**F44.** [verified] Codex CLI headless (`codex exec --json`) emits JSONL events `thread.started`, `turn.started`, `item.*`, `turn.completed`, `turn.failed`; `turn.completed` carries `usage` with `input_tokens`, `cached_input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `reasoning_output_tokens`; no cost field, and per openai/codex issue #17539 (2026-04-12, closed) the usage is cumulative for the thread, not per request. Sources: https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode.md; https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/17539. Cost must be computed from a price table by the caller.

**F45.** [verified] For a maintainer on individual subscriptions, no vendor usage API applies: Anthropic's Admin API "is unavailable for individual accounts" (F9, F10); OpenAI's Usage API needs an organisation Admin key (F11); Copilot metrics need an org or enterprise with the policy enabled (F7). The only per-run usage data available at zero setup cost is the harness's own client-side estimate (F42, F44), which is what kari-website relied on.

**F46.** [verified] kari-website's actual measurement surface, as built: per-session usage JSON (203 records, 180-day retention), per-run logs (139, 30-day retention) ending in `tick exited <code>` so a missing trailer means SIGKILL/OOM, `--status` summing runs/total/mean cost per agent and showing inter-run gaps "recovered from the logs/<name>-<timestamp>.log filenames", and rate-limited Telegram alerts for `usage-limit`, `tick-failed` and stuck locks (`automation/README.md` lines 133-162, 228-241, 243-258). Nothing links a session to the PRs or issues it touched; there is no per-PR, per-class or per-stage record, and the two cadence changes (#635 up, #667 back) were decided on backlog pressure and cost, per the fleet spec decisions log (lines 187-192), not on a stored metric.

### 5. Known pitfalls

**F47.** [verified] DORA names Goodhart's law as its first pitfall: "setting metrics as a goal", followed by "making disparate comparisons" across applications, organisation-wide targets such as "every application must deploy multiple times per day by year's end", siloed accountability that "can lead to friction and finger-pointing", and competitive framing; it recommends applying the metrics per application and in context. Source: https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics-four-keys/.

**F48.** [verified] DORA (2026-06-02) documents token consumption as a target gone wrong: raw token count is "a vanity metric"; "when a metric becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"; leaderboards drove developers to run unnecessary projects, accept low-quality AI code, and hoard effective techniques. Source: https://dora.dev/insights/finding-balance-in-the-era-of-tokenmaxxing/. Any panelator metric that rewards *more* agent activity (sessions, PRs opened, tokens) is exposed to this.

**F49.** [verified] Self-report and perception metrics mismeasure speed: METR's developers believed they were 20% faster while measured 19% slower (F12); acceptance rate tracks perceived, not measured, productivity (F17); GitHub frames its own comparisons as "directional associations" (F8). Over 80% of DORA respondents report AI improved productivity (F2) while the measured RCTs disagree with each other (F13).

**F50.** [verified] Merge rate is confounded as a quality signal: 38% of rejected agent PRs were abandoned by reviewers and 23% were duplicates, so most "failures" say nothing about code quality; task mix (docs 84% vs performance 55%) and per-agent repository populations (Codex 82.59% vs Copilot 43.04%) differ, so cross-agent or cross-class merge rates are not comparable without controlling for task class (F15). The same applies to panelator's rework and escape rates per change class: the class defines the baseline.

**F51.** [verified] CI failures predict non-merge (each one cuts odds about 15%, F15), which makes "time to green" and "CI failures per PR" attractive, but also makes them the most natural target for an agent to game by weakening tests. No primary source quantifying agents weakening tests was reached (OpenAI's chain-of-thought-monitoring post returned 404). kari-website's defence was structural, not measured: coverage is "a ratchet" enforced in CI (`AGENTS.md` line 113; plan and review briefs mention the ratchet, `automation/templates/plan-brief.md` line 47, `review-brief.md` line 25).

**F52.** [verified] Proxy metrics for debt and machinery do not track the thing they stand for: Google found no code or process metric predicted survey-reported technical debt (F18); kari-website's one machinery measurement was a manual three-day count whose remedy was prompt text and whose recurrence was never checked (F24).

**F53.** [verified] Cost figures from harnesses are estimates that can undercount (subagents excluded from `usage`), double-count (resumed sessions), or vanish (crashes) (F43); kari-website's README tells the operator to reconcile against the subscription usage page before trusting totals (F23). A cost-per-PR metric built on them inherits those errors unless the attribution step is designed in.

## What others do

- **Delivery outcomes over activity.** DORA measures deployments (lead time, frequency, fail rate, recovery, rework) and, for AI, recommends adding cost per accepted change and code rework rates while refusing to treat usage or token volume as success (F1, F4, F48). GitHub's and Anthropic's vendor dashboards, by contrast, report activity: suggestions, acceptances, lines, sessions, commits, PRs created (F7, F10), and GitHub itself warns these are directional only (F8).
- **Throughput and instability are read together.** The one consistent AI-era finding is that adoption raises both, so no source treats a throughput gain as success without an instability metric beside it (F2, F5).
- **Rejection reasons are categorised, not just counted.** The agent-PR studies decompose non-merge into reviewer abandonment, duplicates, CI failures, unwanted features and agent failure before drawing conclusions (F15, F16); merge rate alone is treated as uninterpretable (F50).
- **Autonomy is gated by risk class decided in advance, then by green checks.** Renovate, Dependabot, Prow, Mergify and the jade instance all pick the class (patch vs minor, devDependencies, protected paths, author) ahead of time and let CI plus labels decide the merge (F29, F30, F32, F34). None promotes a class on measured history.
- **Where measured outcomes do gate promotion, it is deployments with numeric thresholds and automatic rollback.** Argo Rollouts and Flagger express the rule as metric, interval, count, failure limit, and pause for a human only on an inconclusive result (F31). The AWS design transfers that shape to agents, with hysteresis and immediate demotion on a safety floor, but without data (F33).
- **Demotion signals are per item and immediate.** Retry needed, tests not run, security flag, protected path: each independently blocks self-merge regardless of the standing grade (F34, F33).
- **Collection is from the tracker, one PR at a time.** Timeline events for labels and reviews, the single-PR GET for sizes, workflow runs by `head_sha` for CI; search is avoided for completeness (F37 to F41). Harness cost is taken from the run's own result object, treated as an estimate, and reconciled upstream when an org account allows (F42 to F46).
- **Perception is measured deliberately or not at all.** SPACE insists on it (F6, partially verified); METR and Ziegler show why it must be kept separate from measured speed (F12, F17).

## Open questions for the thinking run

1. **Which "rework" does the ladder use?** DORA's rework is deployment-level (F1); the spec's is fix cycles per PR (F19); the agent-PR studies use CI-failure counts and review rounds (F15). Options: fix cycles from review `state` counts (F38); CI failures per PR from runs by `head_sha` (F40); post-merge reverts or follow-up fixes (closest to DORA). Bears on: F1, F4, F15, F19, F38, F40.
2. **Can a class earn autonomy from history at all with panelator's volumes?** Every deployed ladder gates on class-in-advance plus green checks (F36); the only history-based scheme uses a rolling 50-action window per agent (F33). With a few PRs per class per month, a rolling window may never fill. Options: adopt the risk-class ladder (Renovate shape, F29) and record outcomes for later; adopt AWS-style scoring with a small window and hysteresis; combine class-in-advance with per-item demotion signals (F34). Bears on: F29 to F36, F50.
3. **What are the thresholds, and who sets them?** No source gives a threshold for code-change autonomy; progressive delivery gives the shape (metric, interval, count, failureLimit, inconclusive pauses for a human) (F31). Options: fixed counts ("N consecutive merges with zero fix cycles and zero escapes"), rate with minimum sample, or human promotion only (kari's and jade's position). Bears on: F31, F33, F34.
4. **How is cost attributed to a PR and a stage?** The harness reports per session, estimates only, subagents only in `modelUsage` (F42, F43); kari never linked sessions to PRs (F23, F46). Options: one session per stage per issue (clean attribution, more orchestration tokens); parse `stream-json` by `parent_tool_use_id`; accept session-level cost and divide. Bears on: F23, F42 to F46, F53.
5. **Which metrics are outcomes and which are activity, and are activity metrics stored at all?** DORA and GitHub both caution against activity as success (F4, F8, F48). Options: store activity only as denominators (PRs merged, sessions) never as targets; exclude tokens from any report an agent reads. Bears on: F4, F8, F10, F48.
6. **Where does the record live?** The spec proposes Postgres plus an admin page (section 7); the GitHub timeline already holds label, review and merge events (F39), and the harness result object holds cost (F42). Options: derive on demand from GitHub plus usage JSON (cheap, complete-by-construction if list endpoints are used, F41); ingest into Postgres (needed for escape-rate joins with telemetry). Bears on: F37 to F41, F46.
7. **How is "spec deviation" labelled?** No published metric; the nearest are rejection taxonomies applied by researchers after the fact (F15, F16, F20). Options: the review agent emits a structured finding category (scope / missed criterion / standards) that is counted; or human-only labelling. Bears on: F15, F16, F20.
8. **Is spec churn worth counting with one approver?** NASA's volatility metric assumes a baseline and a change log (F21). Options: count edits to `specs/*.md` after the approval label via the timeline and git; or treat any post-approval edit as an incident with a reason. Bears on: F21, F39.
9. **Should perception be measured when the only human is the maintainer?** SPACE says yes (F6, partially verified); METR shows self-report diverges from measurement (F12, F49). Options: a one-line periodic self-rating alongside the measured numbers; none. Bears on: F6, F12, F17, F49.
10. **What protects "time to green" and CI-failure metrics from test weakening?** No primary data reached (F51). Options: the coverage ratchet and golden-pixel tests as structural guards; a review-agent check that diffs touching tests are flagged; counting test-file deletions as a demotion signal (F34 shape). Bears on: F15, F34, F51.
11. **Is the product-vs-machinery ratio a label count or a path count?** kari used labels set by prompt and measured once by hand (F24, F52). Options: derive from changed paths (`infra/`, `.github/`, automation dirs vs product dirs) at merge time, which needs no labelling; or label at triage, which the spec already requires. Bears on: F24, F37, F52.
12. **Human interventions: what counts?** No published definition with data (F26). Options: `needs-human`-style label events (F39), maintainer-authored review states (F38), Telegram escalations answered, manual stable promotions. Bears on: F26, F38, F39.
