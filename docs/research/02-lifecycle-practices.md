## Question

How do teams running agent-driven development move a change from request to production, and which gate and loop designs have published evidence behind them? Six sub-questions: (1) methods for turning a vague request into an implementable spec with an agent, and what evidence backs their fidelity claims; (2) how plan → implement → review → fix loops are structured, including subagent dispatch, fix-cycle limits and stall escalation, comparing the superpowers skills (designed) with kari-website's issue pipeline (deployed) and recording where the deployed one deviated and why; (3) how automated reviewers run at the PR gate (GitHub Copilot, Anthropic's claude-code-action, OpenAI Codex), what they check, what they can block, false-positive handling and cost; (4) how open-source embedded projects run hardware-in-the-loop in CI; (5) canary/rollback patterns and firmware channel promotion rules in projects shipping OTA to users' devices; (6) published handling of the problem that agents treat tracker items as authoritative.

## Findings

### 1. Requirements extraction

**F1 [verified]** The `grilling` skill (mattpocock-skills 1.2.3) is a structured interview that models the decision space as a **design tree**, asks the whole **frontier** ("every decision whose prerequisites are already settled") per round with a numbered question and a recommended answer each, recomputes the frontier after answers, delegates fact-finding to sub-agents ("Finding *facts* is your job, never the user's ... The *decisions* are the user's"), and terminates when "the frontier is empty: every branch of the design tree visited, nothing left silently assumed. Do not act on it until the user confirms you have reached a shared understanding." The whole skill is 1,987 bytes with no supporting files beyond an `agents/openai.yaml` display stub. Source: `/home/adamd/.claude/plugins/cache/claude-plugins-official/mattpocock-skills/1.2.3/skills/productivity/grilling/SKILL.md`.

**F2 [verified]** The grilling skill's recorded design rationale is in its CHANGELOG, not a separate doc. PR #593 moved it from one-question-at-a-time to rounds with the stated effect "Same 13 questions land in ~3 rounds instead of 13" and made research non-blocking. PR #464 added the confirmation gate and the facts-vs-decisions split because, when another skill ran grilling inside a resolve-the-ticket frame, "explore the codebase instead" "read as license to answer *decisions* autonomously too. Separating the two keeps a grilling agent from racing ahead and answering its own questions." PR #532 generalised it beyond software plans. No measurement of spec fidelity or downstream rework is recorded anywhere in the plugin. Source: `.../mattpocock-skills/1.2.3/CHANGELOG.md` (entries #593, #464, #532); `CONTEXT.md`.

**F3 [verified]** superpowers 6.4.1 `brainstorming` classifies every request as **spike / bounded / architectural** ("When in doubt between two paths, take the heavier one. The ratchet is one-way"), enforces a `<HARD-GATE>` that approval of one stage never approves the next ("Conversational design approval only permits writing the spec; written-spec approval only permits invoking writing-plans"), asks one question per message, proposes 2–3 approaches, writes the spec to `docs/superpowers/specs/YYYY-MM-DD-<topic>-design.md`, runs a four-point spec self-review (placeholders, internal consistency, scope, ambiguity), then a user review gate. It writes back understanding as a note that separates "what they said from assumptions". No evidence for fidelity is cited. Source: `.../superpowers/6.4.1/skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md`.

**F4 [verified]** GitHub spec-kit's `/speckit.clarify` is bounded: "Maximum of 5 total questions across the whole session", scanning a nine-category ambiguity taxonomy (functional scope, domain/data, interaction/UX, non-functional, integration, edge cases, constraints, terminology, completion signals) and selecting "the top 5 by (Impact * Uncertainty) heuristic"; each accepted answer is appended as `- Q: <question> → A: <final answer>` under `## Clarifications / ### Session YYYY-MM-DD` and applied to the affected spec sections. Its spec template mandates prioritised user stories (P1…) each with "Can be fully tested by [specific action] and delivers [specific value]" and **Given/When/Then** acceptance scenarios, `FR-###` requirements with `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` markers, `SC-###` success criteria that are "Measurable", "Technology-agnostic", an Edge Cases section and an Assumptions section. Sources: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/main/templates/commands/clarify.md`; `.../templates/spec-template.md`.

**F5 [verified]** spec-kit's rationale document claims the process yields specs that are "Complete", "Unambiguous", "Testable", "Maintainable", but, as the fetched text shows, "offers no empirical data comparing SDD outcomes to traditional development approaches"; its only number is documentation time (15 minutes vs ~12 hours). Source: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/main/spec-driven.md`.

**F6 [verified]** Kiro's requirements-first flow writes `requirements.md` in **EARS** form ("WHEN a user submits valid registration data THE SYSTEM SHALL create a new user account"), then `design.md`, then `tasks.md`, with a human confirmation at each phase ("Confirm when requirements meet your needs", "Confirm the design is feasible"). It asserts EARS requirements are "unambiguous and testable, easy to translate into test cases, traceable through implementation" with no cited evidence. Source: `https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/requirements-first/`.

**F7 [verified]** Anthropic's Claude Code best-practices page recommends, for larger features, "have Claude interview you first ... using the `AskUserQuestion` tool ... Keep interviewing until we've covered everything, then write a complete spec to SPEC.md", then "start a fresh session to execute it". Its definition of a good spec: "self-contained: they name the files and interfaces involved, state what is out of scope, and end with an end-to-end verification step that proves the feature works." It also says "If you could describe the diff in one sentence, skip the plan." Source: `https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices` (sections "Let Claude interview you", "Explore first, then plan, then code").

**F8 [verified]** kari-website has no spec stage. Readiness is judged per issue at pick time: "is the desired outcome unambiguous enough to build without product decisions you'd be guessing at? If not, post ONE comment asking the specific blocking questions and add the `needs-clarification` label ... Never comment without labelling." The plan brief's required fields are Approach, Files, Tests ("Name the cases, not just 'add tests'"), Risks and gotchas, Out of scope, and a conditional Design intent paragraph. Sources: `/home/adamd/Projects/kari-website/automation/agents/issue-pipeline.md` lines ~470–490; `automation/templates/plan-brief.md`.

### 2. Plan, implement, review loops

**F10 [verified]** superpowers 6.4.1 `subagent-driven-development` (SDD): "Fresh subagent per task + task review (spec + quality) + broad final review". Fix loop: "A fix round is one fix dispatch plus one scoped re-review. Five rounds maximum per task." Rounds 1–3 resume the original implementer with findings verbatim; rounds 4–5 "dispatch a fresh implementer on a more capable model" because "A loop that survives three resumes usually means the implementer cannot see its own problem — fresh eyes and a capability bump in one move." At the cap, "The breaker": the controller adjudicates each finding (park with ruling, or rule on load-bearing ones) and "Adjudicate only at the cap. Adjudicating earlier to end a loop is pre-judging with a different name." Minor findings never enter the loop. The final whole-branch review gets "ONE fix subagent with the complete findings list — not one fixer per finding ... a real session's final-review fix wave cost more than all its tasks combined", then "exactly one scoped re-review", and "There is no second fix wave". Source: `.../superpowers/6.4.1/skills/subagent-driven-development/SKILL.md` ("4. The fix loop", "Final Review").

**F11 [verified]** SDD's stop rule for autonomy: "Rulings, not stalls. A running plan does not wait on a human ... Record every decision in the ledger as `Ruling: <what you decided> — <why> — <what it costs if wrong>`". Only four things stop it: "an irreversible or destructive operation; a security-sensitive action; a side effect outside this worktree that norms say you ask about first (a merge, a push to a shared branch, a publish); and a plan so broken that every path forward is a guess." Progress lives in a git-ignored ledger (`.superpowers/sdd/<plan>/progress.md`) so a compacted or resumed session does not redo tasks. Source: same file, header and "Setup".

**F12 [verified]** SDD model policy: "Use the least powerful model that can handle each role"; mechanical 1–2-file tasks on a cheap model, integration on a standard model, "Architecture and design tasks: use the most capable available model. The final whole-branch review is one of these"; fix-loop escalation "at least one tier above the implementer that got stuck"; "Always specify the model explicitly ... An omitted model inherits your session's model — often the most capable and most expensive". Caveat recorded: "Turn count beats token price ... the cheapest models routinely take 2-3× the turns on multi-step work — costing more overall. Use a mid-tier model as the floor for reviewers and for implementers working from prose descriptions." Source: same file, "Model Selection".

**F13 [verified]** `executing-plans` (inline variant) drops per-task reviewers, keeps "One fresh-context review of the whole branch at the end" on "the most capable available model", allows "ONE fix pass" with each fix "RED→GREEN", and states "A fully specified plan makes inline execution transcription plus testing: it runs well on a mid-tier session model". The ledger format is shared with SDD so a plan can "change executors mid-flight". Source: `.../superpowers/6.4.1/skills/executing-plans/SKILL.md`.

**F14 [verified]** `requesting-code-review`'s reviewer template returns Strengths / Critical / Important / Minor / Assessment, adds a "Declined to judge" list ("nothing you set aside is dropped silently"), frames "The spec is a vision document ... a spec's silence is not permission", and forbids the reviewer from dispatching sub-reviewers ("its verdict counts for nothing"). `receiving-code-review` requires "Verify before implementing" and pushback with technical reasoning; it forbids performative agreement. Sources: `.../requesting-code-review/code-reviewer.md`; `.../receiving-code-review/SKILL.md`.

**F15 [verified]** `writing-plans` fixes the plan artifact: header with `**Spec:**` pointer, `## Global Constraints` copied verbatim from the spec, `## Review Focus` ("The five input classes or failure modes the spec implies but no task's tests exercise"), per-task `**Files:**` with exact paths/line ranges, `**Interfaces:** Consumes/Produces`, and steps with `Run:`/`Expected:` lines; a "No Placeholders" list ("TBD", "add appropriate error handling", "Similar to Task N" are plan failures); a four-point self-review. Source: `.../superpowers/6.4.1/skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md`.

**F16 [verified]** Anthropic's best-practices page gives a four-level verification ladder: in-prompt ("run the tests after implementing"), `/goal` ("A separate evaluator re-checks it after every turn"), a Stop hook ("a deterministic gate ... blocks the turn from ending until it passes. Stop input covers the cap on consecutive blocks"), and "a verification subagent ... so the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it." On review it warns: "A reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound ... Tell the reviewer to flag only gaps that affect correctness or the stated requirements." On stalls: "After two failed corrections, `/clear` and write a better initial prompt." Source: `https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices`.

**F17 [verified]** Claude Code's `/goal` is "a wrapper around a session-scoped prompt-based Stop hook": after each turn "your configured small fast model" returns Not yet met / Met / Impossible; "If Claude keeps answering the evaluator without making progress (no tool use for several turns in a row), Claude Code stops the loop"; transient errors get "three automatic retries" then pause; auth failure, exhausted credits, uncompactable context overflow, or unavailable model clear the goal. It runs headless: `claude -p "/goal ..."`. The evaluator "does not call tools, so it can only judge what Claude has already surfaced in the conversation." Source: `https://code.claude.com/docs/en/goal`.

**F18 [verified]** Anthropic, "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (2025-11-26): an initializer agent writes `init.sh`, a progress file and a feature-list JSON with each feature marked passing/failing ("It is unacceptable to remove or edit tests"); coding agents work "one feature at a time", which was "critical to addressing the agent's tendency to do too much at once"; agents verify end-to-end with browser automation "as a human user would". Observed failures: declaring the project complete prematurely, running out of context leaving features "half-implemented and undocumented", and marking features done "without proper testing". Source: `https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents`.

**F19 [verified]** Anthropic, "Harness design for long-running application development" (2026-03-24): three roles, planner / generator / evaluator; the evaluator does browser QA via Playwright MCP and grades against criteria; self-grading failed because "agents tend to respond by confidently praising the work—even when, to a human observer, the quality is obviously mediocre", whereas "tuning a standalone evaluator to be skeptical turns out to be far more tractable." Agents hand off "via files". Loop length in the design experiment was "5 to 15 iterations per generation". Cost: solo agent 20 min / $9 vs full harness 6 h / $200 on one app; on another, planner $0.46, build rounds $71.08 + $36.89 + $5.88, QA rounds $3.24 + $3.09 + $4.06, total $124.70. Source: `https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps`.

**F20 [verified]** kari-website's **designed** pipeline (spec dated 2026-08-19, status "approved"): dispatcher on cron every 15 min; `every: 1h`; Phase A tends PRs (fix agent on CI fail, visual-review findings, review gate adds `agent:reviewed`, serial squash merge), Phase B picks work ("List open issues", per-issue readiness, complexity classification), Phase C files issues from worker reports; worker briefs "explicitly invoke superpowers skills (test-driven-development; systematic-debugging ...; verification-before-completion)"; merge gate "CI green + visual findings addressed + automated code review clean (chosen over merge-on-green and human-merge)". No validation stage, no Telegram, no shortlist script, no fix-cycle cap, model "split was per-issue on the worker itself". Source: `/home/adamd/Projects/kari-website/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-automation-fleet-design.md`.

### 3. Agent code review at the PR gate

**F24 [verified]** GitHub Copilot code review: reviews "any language" for "bugs, security vulnerabilities, and style inconsistencies"; "Copilot can submit an approving review that satisfies your repository's required-approval rule" (public preview) but does not request changes or block; automatic review via user settings or organisation rulesets on PR open, draft→ready, optionally each push; estimated consumption "$0.05–$1 USD (Lite effort) to $0.25–$5 USD (Balanced effort), excluding GitHub Actions minutes", increasing "with pull request size and repository custom instructions"; "Model switching is not supported"; false positives: "Always validate Copilot's feedback carefully. Supplement Copilot's feedback with a human review", plus repository custom instructions. Source: `https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/code-review/code-review`.

**F25 [verified]** anthropics/claude-code-action v1: configured by `prompt` + `claude_args`; the documented automatic-review workflow runs on `pull_request: [opened, synchronize]` with `--allowedTools "mcp__github_inline_comment__create_inline_comment,Bash(gh pr comment:*),Bash(gh pr diff:*),Bash(gh pr view:*)"` and posts inline comments; it issues no approval and no request-changes; inline comments without `confirmed: true` are "buffered and classified after the session ends (real review comments post, test/probe comments are filtered)"; `track_progress: true` adds a mutable tracking comment. Neither README nor FAQ states a per-run cost; the FAQ only says "Monitor Claude's token usage to avoid hitting API limits". Sources: `https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code-action/blob/main/docs/solutions.md`; `.../README.md`; `.../docs/faq.md`.

**F26 [verified]** claude-code-action security model: "The action can only be triggered by users with write access to the repository" for issue/PR/comment/review/`workflow_run` events; "By default, GitHub Apps and bots cannot trigger this action", and `allowed_bots` entries "are not checked for repository permissions"; `allowed_non_write_users` is marked "RISKY"; content is sanitised ("stripping HTML comments, invisible characters, markdown image alt text, hidden HTML attributes, and HTML entities, but new bypass techniques may emerge"); comment authors can be allow/deny-listed (`include_comments_by_actor`, `exclude_comments_by_actor`); for PRs it restores `.claude/`, `CLAUDE.md`, `.mcp.json` etc. from the base branch so a PR cannot supply the agent's own configuration; "Do not check out an untrusted ref into the workspace root before this action." Source: `https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code-action/blob/main/docs/security.md`.

**F27 [verified]** OpenAI Codex code review: triggered by `@codex review` or per-repository "Automatic review"; it "reviews the pull request diff, follows your repository guidance, and posts a standard GitHub code review focused on serious issues" and "flags only P0 and P1 issues"; repository guidance goes in `AGENTS.md` under `## Code Review Rules` and should "Avoid mechanical checks in CI"; a follow-up `@codex fix the P1 issue` is supported; configuring automatic reviews needs "GitHub push or admin permission". The doc does not say whether Codex approves or requests changes, nor who may invoke `@codex review`, nor fork handling. Billing: "Code Review usage applies only when Codex runs reviews through GitHub ... Reviews run locally or outside of GitHub count toward your general usage limits"; no per-review price is published. Sources: `https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/third-party/github`; `https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing`.

## What others do

Descriptive; each pattern points to the findings that support it.

1. **Interview, then write a spec, then start a fresh context.** Three independent sources converge on an interview-then-artifact shape: grilling's frontier rounds ending in a confirmation gate (F1–F2), brainstorming's hard gate between design, spec and plan (F3), Anthropic's "interview me ... write SPEC.md ... start a fresh session" (F7). Bounds differ: spec-kit caps at five questions chosen by impact × uncertainty (F4); grilling stops when the frontier is empty (F1).
2. **Spec templates make acceptance criteria executable-looking.** Given/When/Then (spec-kit, F4), EARS WHEN/THE SYSTEM SHALL (Kiro, F6), measurable technology-agnostic success criteria and explicit out-of-scope (F4, F7), `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` markers for unknowns (F4). Nobody publishes outcome evidence for any of them (F9).
3. **Plans carry exact values so a cheaper executor can transcribe.** writing-plans' Files/Interfaces/Expected lines and "No Placeholders" (F15); kari's plan brief "Concrete beats complete: name real files, real functions, real test cases" (F8); SDD and executing-plans both say a fully specified plan runs on a mid-tier model (F12–F13).
4. **Separate the grader from the worker.** Fresh-context reviewer subagents (F10, F14, F16), a separate evaluator model in `/goal` (F17), Anthropic's planner/generator/evaluator with the explicit finding that self-grading praises mediocre work (F19), kari's fresh-session validator (F43) and adversarial review brief (F22).
5. **Bound the fix loop and escalate by changing something, not by repeating.** SDD: five rounds, resume for three, then fresh implementer one tier up, then adjudicate (F10). kari: two surviving cycles → plan agent → human (F21). Anthropic: two failed corrections → clear context and re-prompt (F16); `/goal` stops after several no-tool turns and three retries (F17). Every source treats "same input again" as the failure mode to avoid.
6. **One fix wave at the end, not one fixer per finding.** SDD's final review dispatches one fixer with all findings and one scoped re-review, citing a session where the fix wave "cost more than all its tasks combined" (F10); executing-plans allows one fix pass (F13).
7. **Reviewers are told what not to report.** "Do NOT report style nits" (F22), "flag only gaps that affect correctness" (F16), "P0 and P1 issues" only (F27), "Declined to judge" lists so omissions are explicit (F14).
8. **Vendor reviewers advise; blocking comes from branch protection or the orchestrator.** Copilot can approve but not block; claude-code-action and Codex post comments (F24–F27, F29). kari enforces its gate with a label in a prompt (F29–F30).
9. **HIL CI = build on a hosted runner, flash and assert on a self-hosted runner labelled by board, with serial-regex assertions, JUnit output and per-test timeouts.** ESP-IDF/esp-dsp on GitLab tags (F31–F32), Golioth on GitHub Actions labels `[is_active, has_<board>]` (F33), Zephyr's hardware map plus fixtures and quarantine lists (F34). Flake handling is explicit (rerunfailures, quarantine, retries).
10. **Firmware channels are branches/URLs; promotion is calendar-driven and human-run.** HA first-Wednesday stable after a one-week beta with milestone-tagged cherry-picks (F38); ESPHome third-Wednesday release after betas cut from `dev` with cherry-picks (F39); Tasmota and WLED expose development/nightly vs release channels (F40–F41). No metric-gated promotion found (F42).
11. **Service canaries use a metric threshold with a failure count, then automatic abort to the stable version; inconclusive pauses for a human.** Argo Rollouts `failureLimit` → abort/Degraded, inconclusive → paused (F36); Flagger `threshold` → rollback (F37).
12. **Agent-run pipelines gate on who asked, not on whether the ask is right.** Write-access checks, bot allow-lists, hidden-character stripping, requester-cannot-approve (F26, F46). Content validation exists only in kari-website and only as prose (F43, F47).
13. **Deployed systems drift toward spend control and away from the design.** Nine cadence edits in 23 days, model policy moved to plan-first + cheap implementation, plugin retired for per-tick injection cost (F21). Every structural change in kari's log has a recorded incident or cost reason.
14. **Provenance travels as labels and dual signals because credentials are shared.** `automation` vs `tooling`, human-only `priority`/`user-feedback`, `agent/` branch + `agent-pr` label (F45); process-spec section 4.3's "bot-authored only" is the scripted version of the same idea.

## Open questions for the thinking run

1. **Fix-cycle cap and escalation ladder.** Evidence leaves open: kari's 2 cycles → plan agent → human (F21) vs SDD's 5 rounds with a model bump at round 4 and adjudication at the cap (F10) vs Anthropic's "after two failed corrections, clear and re-prompt" (F16). Options: a small cap with a planning pass; a larger cap with model escalation; context reset as the first escalation. None of the three publishes rework data to choose by; process-spec section 7's rework-rate metric would be the first such data.
2. **Review granularity.** Per-task review inside the worker (SDD, F10) vs one review per PR at the gate (kari, F22) vs both (SDD's task review plus final review). Bears on cost (F10's fix-wave anecdote, F19's QA-round costs of $3–4) and on where spec deviation is caught. Open.
3. **Who decides when the plan and the code disagree.** SDD's "Rulings, not stalls" with a ledger (F11) vs kari's "STOP: do not guess at product decisions" (F22) vs process-spec's one human gate. Options: rulings allowed within the spec, stop only on product decisions; stop on any ambiguity; rulings always, surfaced for review.
4. **Blocking mechanism for the review gate.** Branch protection with a bot approval (F24, F29), an orchestrator-checked label (F30), or a Stop-hook/deterministic script (F16). Process-spec requires "enforced in the script, not in a prompt" for issue authority; whether the review gate gets the same treatment is open.
5. **Own reviewer vs vendor reviewer at the PR gate.** Vendor reviewers are advisory, model-fixed (Copilot) or plan-metered (Codex), and gate on actor identity (F24–F27, F46); kari's own reviewer is spec-aware and cheap to tune but unscripted (F22). Options: own reviewer only; vendor as a second opinion on sensitive paths; vendor only.
6. **Spec template.** Given/When/Then with `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` and measurable SC (F4), EARS (F6), or the lighter "files, interfaces, out-of-scope, end-to-end verification step" (F7) plus writing-plans' Global Constraints / Review Focus (F15). No fidelity evidence exists for any (F9); process-spec's spec-deviation and spec-churn metrics would supply it.
7. **Interview bound.** Five questions by impact × uncertainty (F4) vs frontier-empty (F1) vs one question per message (F3). Bears on how the Telegram grilling bot paces a session.
8. **Validation stage: scripted or prose.** kari's validator is a process rule with no script check (F43); process-spec 4.2 requires a filing agent. Open: which parts (freshness SHA diff, verdict presence, fresh-session requirement) can be scripted, and whether a `VALIDATED` comment should be machine-readable.
9. **HIL runner shape.** Build on hosted/CI, flash on a self-hosted runner labelled by board (F31–F34) is the common shape; panelator's laptop is both agent host and HIL rig (process-spec 6.1). Open: label scheme, per-test and per-job timeouts (observed 600 s / 30 min at Golioth, 1 h at esp-dsp), flake policy (quarantine vs rerun), and whether HIL runs on every PR or on merge/schedule as Golioth does.
10. **Firmware stable promotion rule.** All four OTA projects promote on a calendar by a human (F38–F42); process-spec says stable stays human "until the HIL rig and the escape-rate metric justify automating it". Open: what threshold, over what window, and whether a calendar floor (minimum days in beta) remains even when the metric passes.
11. **Canary tooling at small scale.** Argo Rollouts and Flagger assume Kubernetes and a metrics provider (F36–F37); the backend's deployment target is not fixed in the process spec. Open: adopt one of these, or reproduce "N failed checks over interval → abort to stable; inconclusive → pause" in deploy scripts.
12. **Reviewer false positives.** Sources handle them by instruction ("only P0/P1", "no style nits", "flag only correctness") and by letting the fixer dismiss with a reasoned comment (F16, F22, F27). Open: whether dismissals are counted (as a reviewer-quality metric) and who may dismiss a security-review finding.
13. **Cost per PR for review.** Only Copilot publishes a range (F24); Codex and claude-code-action do not (F25, F27–F28); Anthropic's harness post gives $3–4 per QA round (F19). Open: measure locally (process-spec section 7 cost metric) rather than rely on vendor numbers.
